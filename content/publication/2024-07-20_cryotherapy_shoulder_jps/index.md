## Lay summary

Cold therapy (cryotherapy) is widely used in sport to manage injuries and help athletes recover. Some people worry that cooling a joint might dull its "position sense", the body's awareness of where a limb is without looking, which could raise the risk of injury if an athlete returns to play straight afterwards. Little research has looked at this in the shoulder. We tested it in recreationally active adults using a device that circulates cold water around the shoulder, either on its own or combined with rhythmic squeezing (intermittent compression). Each person tried both treatments, in random order. Before and after each treatment, we measured how accurately they could move their arm to a target angle. Neither treatment caused a meaningful change in accuracy, and any differences were small. This suggests cryotherapy, with or without compression, is unlikely to harm shoulder position sense, so it remains a reasonable option for athletes before they return to activity.

## Lay summary

Sports and exercise therapists make decisions every day about how to assess, treat and rehabilitate injured and active people. Evidence-based practice is an approach that helps them make those decisions well. It brings together three things: the best available research, the therapist's own clinical experience, and the needs, values and preferences of the person being treated. This chapter, written for students and practising therapists, explains what evidence-based practice is and why it matters. It describes how to turn a clinical problem into a clear question, how to search for relevant research, and how to judge whether a study is trustworthy and useful. It also looks at how to apply research findings in everyday practice and how to check whether a treatment has worked. The aim is to help therapists feel confident using research in their day-to-day work, so that the people they care for receive safe, effective and up-to-date treatment.

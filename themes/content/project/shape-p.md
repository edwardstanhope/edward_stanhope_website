**STRIDE-P** (Stoke-on-Trent Inclusive Delivery of Exercise for Parkinson's) and **SHAPE-P** (Staffordshire Health and Physical Activity Engagement for Parkinson's) tested whether a structured, phased exercise programme is feasible, safe and acceptable for people living with Parkinson's. The project is now complete, and the findings will inform a larger trial and future service delivery.

### Who it's for

Adults aged 30 to 75 with mild to moderate Parkinson's (Hoehn & Yahr stages 1 to 3) who are not currently meeting recommended physical activity levels.

### The programme

- **Weeks 1–4, Learn:** weekly face-to-face group sessions with an exercise specialist at the University of Staffordshire
- **Weeks 5–10, Transition:** weekly live online group sessions with the same coach and group
- **Weeks 11–18, Own it:** independent home exercise guided by exercise recordings
- Three behaviour change consultations to support goal setting and motivation

Each session combines aerobic, resistance and balance training. Physical function and quality of life are assessed at baseline, at 18 weeks, and at a 3-month follow-up.

### Final findings

**Feasibility and engagement**

- 19 people enrolled across three cohorts (79% of the recruitment target), and 78% of supervised sessions were attended
- 84% of participants attended at least 70% of their sessions
- Remote sessions were as well attended as face-to-face ones (77% vs 79%), although fewer people met the attendance threshold once sessions moved online (61% vs 95%)
- Participants progressed steadily through the programme, for example air squat repetitions rose by 79% from the first to the final session

**Physical function**

- Sit-to-stand performance improved by 6 repetitions (p < 0.001), and Timed Up and Go was 0.8 seconds faster (p = 0.017)
- Six-minute walk distance rose by 36 m, but this was not statistically significant
- Balance did not change significantly, and grip strength fell by 3.5 kg, so both will be looked at closely in future work

**Beyond the programme**

- Participants described greater confidence, motivation and acceptance of their diagnosis
- Many kept exercising afterwards, from joining a gym to setting up a home gym
- 10 of 15 completers formed a peer support group, and one participant now runs regular online sessions for former participants

Participant feedback shaped five priorities for the next version: clearer participant information, more support when moving to independent exercise, more balance training, better home exercise videos, and measuring wellbeing, confidence and social connection.

The findings were presented at the [CHAD 10 Year Celebration Conference](/talk/shape-p-final-findings/) in November 2026. Next, the phased model is being extended to multiple sclerosis (SHAPE-MS), breast cancer (SHAPE-BCa) and other long-term conditions.

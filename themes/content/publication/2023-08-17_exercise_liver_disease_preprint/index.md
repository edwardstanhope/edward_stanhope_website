## Lay summary

Long-term liver disease often develops in stages: inflammation, then scarring (fibrosis), then severe scarring (cirrhosis), and in some people liver cancer. Exercise is known to benefit general health, but it is less clear whether it can slow or reverse this process. We brought together all the studies we could find that compared exercise with little or no exercise in people or animals with liver disease. We found 18 studies in humans and 29 in animals. Only one study directly showed that exercise could reverse fatty liver disease and reduce progression to cirrhosis. However, when we combined the results, exercise lowered blood levels of liver enzymes such as ALT and GGT, which rise when the liver is damaged. In animals, exercise also reduced liver fat and liver weight. This suggests exercise may help protect the liver, but longer studies are needed to show whether it can prevent cirrhosis and liver cancer.
